GPS Error Sources — Educational Reference
Signal Delay
Ionospheric & tropospheric delays slow GPS signals, causing position offset.
Multipath
Signals reflecting off buildings arrive at multiple times, skewing position.
Satellite Geometry
Poor satellite configuration (high DOP) amplifies measurement errors.
Receiver Noise
Electronic noise in GPS chips introduces random offset in measured position.

📚 Educational Vector Calculus Demonstrations — These are mathematical demonstrations of vector calculus concepts relevant to navigation and field analysis. They are clearly labelled as educational — not claims about how commercial GPS routing systems work.
∇
Gradient — ∇f
Direction of greatest increase of a scalar field
∇f = (∂f/∂x)î + (∂f/∂y)ĵ

In GPS navigation: ∇f represents the direction of steepest increase in elevation or signal strength field. Used in terrain-aware routing to avoid steep gradients.

∇·
Divergence — ∇ · F
Net outward flow from a point in a vector field
∇·F = ∂Fx/∂x + ∂Fy/∂y

In navigation analysis: positive divergence indicates traffic spreading out (highway junction); negative divergence indicates traffic converging (bottleneck). Useful in traffic flow modelling.

∇×
Curl — ∇ × F
Local rotational behaviour of a vector field
(∇×F)_z = ∂Fy/∂x − ∂Fx/∂y

In navigation: curl of the velocity field indicates rotational traffic patterns (roundabouts, circular flyovers). Non-zero curl identifies turning flow regions.

∫
Line Integral — ∫C F · dr
Work done by a vector field along a path
∫C F·dr = Σ Fᵢ · Δrᵢ

In navigation: ∫C F·dr represents the total effort (work against resistance) along a route. Lower line integral value means a more efficient path through a resistance field (wind, terrain, traffic).
